Historical agent conclusions are leads, not independent validation of results. Table numbers changed repeatedly: the older “Tables 5/6/7” normally mean CPF sensitivity, trial stability, and timing, which are **Tables VI/VII/VIII in the submitted manuscript**.

**Original method and rejection.** GS-LSS addresses novel-view degradation when camera coverage is biased: missing angular sectors, discontinuous paths, and aerial-to-ground view changes. Its CPF learns a continuous field from aggregated in-training geometry; LSS uses that field to regularize stochastic Gaussian optimization. The original narrative emphasized distribution matching and learned sampling. Reviewers challenged the mathematical justification, the reliability of CPF targets derived from already biased 3DGS geometry, the separation of contributions, baseline fairness, hyperparameter sensitivity, and computational cost. The surviving rejection letter is `others/TVCG-2026-04-0389.txt`; older run paths and memory describe the experiments leading to the original submission.

**4–11 June: speed, diagnosis, and a second training recipe.** The principal local conversation is `019e9099-9040-7920-a669-fdd4ce9e2f93`. It contains 1,823 extracted messages and 15 compaction records. Work moved from the older `train_experimental_branch3.py` recipe to the faster `train_experimental_branch3_mc.py` path. The fast implementation reduced CPF work, used rendered geometry rather than the older depth-heavy supervision path, and introduced bounded, observation-aware perturbations.

Several conclusions from this period matter for future experiments. Scale/opacity regularization was a major confound in early comparisons. A CPF-disabled variant was not automatically equivalent to official 3DGS-MCMC. Restricting densification too early reduced outdoor capacity; the production schedule restored a longer densification window. The mean/pull and learned-noise-head switches were explored but are disabled by default in the shipped fast recipe. A speed improvement alone therefore does not establish that the complete paper-described method has been preserved.

The local Git history preserves these changes through HEAD `02fd14e`: fast CPF work around 5 June, densification fixes around 8 June, floater handling around 9 June, and room-specific multi-view batching/dual-checkpoint evaluation around 11 June. The present working tree contains further uncommitted changes, so HEAD alone cannot reproduce its behavior.

The remote 8 June conversation `019ea73a-e3ea-72b0-8baf-d8d24792b0b1` concerns environment setup, gsplat, depth-free CPF, and CPU DBSCAN fallback. It contains short smoke-run evidence, not a replacement for the main paper runs.

**10–19 June: per-scene tuning and recovery of the original quality.** Claude memory records a long series of same-scene probes: anti-aliasing, depth loss, density/capacity, noise scale, observation gates, field-refresh frequency, field color, pseudo-view supervision, and CPF-guided densification. Many were rejected or kept disabled. In particular, an apparent gain from refreshing CPF more often did not survive a later same-seed comparison. Room benefited from three random views; that result did not transfer uniformly to other scenes.

The older slow recipe remained competitive with or superior to the faster one on some scenes and angles. The accepted accounting eventually pooled FAST and SLOW outputs as GS-LSS. The submitted Tables I and II now match independent per-metric selection from that heterogeneous pool. Earlier memory sometimes refers instead to best-PSNR selection; the current arithmetic, rather than those ambiguous descriptions, determines the provenance reported in this audit.

WSL acted as a secondary training worker, with checkpoints mirrored back to the local repo. It suffered render hangs, worker/watchdog matching errors, and desktop responsiveness problems. Some jobs saved a checkpoint before rendering failed; absence of metrics did not necessarily mean training produced no model. Conversely, a CSV header or a stale watchdog signal did not mean a run succeeded. These incidents explain why completion must be checked through the exact checkpoint, metrics row, and log together.

**17–23 June: main tables, aerial failures, ablations, and timing.** The memory files `gslss-tables12-met-framing1.md`, `gslss-table3-aerial.md`, and `gslss-tables567-efficiency-goal.md` separate three issues that were sometimes conflated: reproducing favorable historical main-table values, finding a faster fixed recipe, and collecting new ablations. Tables I/II could be supported by selecting existing models; the aerial-to-ground GS-LSS values remained below the paper values in located runs.

Ablation work moved between the original Train+Truck 60° protocol and later Truck-only discontinuous runs. Those are different experiments. Twelve local `tnt60_table5` runs preserve a checkpoint-backed sensitivity study, while the submitted sensitivity table combines some of those results with older CSV-only results. The later `tables567` runs do not establish the five independent Train+Truck trials printed in the submitted Table VII.

Timing was measured several ways. The retained clean baseline runs used `hust2` GPU2, native `--eval`, and an external timer. The retained GS-LSS “clean_shipped” run used local GPU1, Truck discontinuous, and the training-loop timer. Old notes loosely call the baseline runs discontinuous, but their launcher and saved `cfg_args` do not supply that split. The paper's final ratios preserve this mixed comparison. New timing on Bonsai was also attempted; it is a separate artifact family.

This period also includes a GPU/UVM failure and recovery work. An early explanation that the method inherently required a newer GPU was superseded by a data-conversion/DBSCAN portability fix. Do not resurrect the abandoned explanation or historical GPU-routing rules. Current experiments must follow the user's present policy: GS-LSS locally on GPU1; baselines on `hust2` GPU3, with local GPU1 as fallback.

**2–4 July: remote supplemental experiments and repeated changes of scope.** The long remote conversation `019efcc9-edea-7a72-8469-28af793e0d48` has 6,916 extracted messages, 70 compaction records, and 177 final answers. It covers CPF reliability, FFR, split-location tests, component ablations, efficiency, watchdogs, and paper revision. The local code/output collection was imported selectively to `hust2`; directory names beginning `hust10_…_20260703` document that direction of transfer.

Early CPF-logit diagnostics did not justify a broad positive calibration/reliability claim. Subsequent work emphasized filtered observed support and bounded diagnostics. Split-location experiments eventually included four centers for Bonsai and Garden with matched 3DGS comparisons. Better FFR did not consistently coincide with better image quality, especially on Garden. Some Truck results were sensitive to checkpoint iteration and densification. These findings limit broad claims of geometry dominance or split robustness.

The supplemental component study evolved from Bonsai+Flowers to Bonsai+Garden, then toward a tighter Bonsai study. Later runs isolated gate, DBSCAN, normal/color terms, mean/pull, and stochastic settings. The surviving checkpoints are useful, but they belong to different revisions, schedules, and settings. Their presence does not back the submitted MatrixCity component table.

A useful chronology anchor is the remote final answer at line 48253, which records completion of Garden matched split-center baselines. Lines 49593 and 49917 record the later decision to concentrate the paper on Bonsai FFR and a merged component table. Historical decisions to retain main-table data unchanged were editorial scope decisions, not successful re-verification of missing models.

**What to carry forward.** Preserve the original evidence and distinguish four tasks: reproducing a selected model's rendering; reproducing a reported aggregate; retraining a specified recipe; and demonstrating a causal mechanism through a matched ablation. They require different evidence. The present audit closes much of the first two for Tables I/II, identifies usable later ablation models, and records what is still missing. It does not convert heterogeneous selected results into a fixed-recipe benchmark or turn a paper-only value into a checkpoint-backed result.
