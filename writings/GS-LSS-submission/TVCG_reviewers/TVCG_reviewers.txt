Associate Editor: 2
Comments to the Author:
This paper proposes GS-LSS, a stochastic optimization framework for 3D Gaussian Splatting under biased viewpoint distributions. It combines a Continuous Probability Field—built from aggregated training view geometry—with guided Gaussian updates and a learned variance module for adaptive random perturbations to address perspective overfitting.

The reviewers acknowledged the following strengths of this paper:
1) "The connection between the method design and the experiments. The evaluation covers 16 scenes from five datasets and three types of viewpoint bias." (Reviewer 3)
2) "Aggregating geometric information across training views gives the optimization additional guidance beyond the loss from an individual image. Combining this guidance with adaptive stochastic updates is well motivated."* (Reviewer 3)
3) "Keeping the standard 3DGS renderer at inference is also useful, although training is more expensive." (Reviewer 3)

However, the reviewers raised major concerns regarding:
1) "Supervision is obtained from RGB, depth, and normals rendered by the same Gaussian model... Eq. (21) and (22) cannot generally be satisfied simultaneously," and Langevin claims lack exact noise-drift relationships. (Reviewer 1)
2) "PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS on held-out images do not establish better global scene structure," and critical ablations (CPF update mode, loss components, gating) are missing. (Reviewer 1)
3) "The dependence of CPF on the quality of the geometry produced during training. Errors in this geometry may affect the guidance provided by CPF." (Reviewer 3)

The authors are therefore required to address and clarify these issues in a subsequent submission. The current recommendation is: Major Revision.

Reviewer Comments


Reviewer: 1

Recommendation: Reject

Comments:
This paper presents GS-LSS, a stochastic optimization framework for 3D Gaussian Splatting under biased viewpoint distributions. The method constructs a Continuous Probability Field from intermediate 3DGS geometry and uses its gradients to guide Gaussian relocation and reshaping, while a learned variance module controls stochastic perturbations.
1. Its supervision is obtained from RGB, depth, and normals rendered by the same Gaussian model trained on biased views. The paper does not isolate this effect through comparisons with fixed SfM support, external depth, or oracle geometry.
2. Equation (21) assigns an isotropic Gaussian probability around each point, while Equation (22) forces the probability gradient to align with an oriented surface normal. Thus, the stated probability model and normal constraint cannot generally be satisfied simultaneously.
3. Equation (17) does not correspond to Langevin sampling of Equation (28), since the learned drift and variance do not satisfy the required noise-drift relationship and no correction step is used. The claimed methodological distinction from heuristic stochastic 3DGS is therefore limited.
4. PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS on held-out images do not establish better global scene structure. The proposed FFR metric measures distance from sparse training-view SfM points and may classify legitimate surfaces outside the observed support as floaters. Evaluation against ground-truth depth, point clouds, meshes, or rendered depth is needed.
5. The exact view counts, random seeds, discontinuous-region selection, per-scene results, and variance across splits are not reported. Five repeated trials on one setting do not address split sensitivity.
6. The paper does not study fixed versus jointly updated CPF, positive-only versus free-space supervision, the individual color and normal losses, observation-aware gating, λ s ​ , λ ϵ ​ , CPF start time, sampling density, or performance under standard well-covered viewpoints. Without these results, neither the source of the improvement nor the risk of over-regularization is established.

Reviewer: 2

Recommendation: Author Should Prepare A Major Revision For A Second Review

Comments:
This paper proposes an observed-support regularized learned stochastic optimization framework to address the issue of perspective overfitting in 3D Gaussian Splatting when trained with biased viewpoints. I recommend a major revision to enhance clarity and provide a more thorough discussion of its method and experiments.

Here are a few points for the improvement:

1. The introduction uses complex and lengthy sentences that can make it challenging for readers to quickly grasp the core issue and the paper's contribution. For example, in the problem statement, The problem of "perspective overfitting" is critical but could be introduced more accessibly.

2. The Related Work section covers relevant areas. But it tends to describe existing methods rather than critically analyzing their fundamental limitations that GS-LSS aims to overcome. The comparison with MCMC-based methods, in particular, could be sharper to highlight the proposed method's novelty.

3. The experimental section presents quantitative and qualitative results. However, the analysis sometimes states what the results show (e.g., "GS-LSS achieves the best performance") without explaining why the proposed method performs better at a deeper, mechanistic level. My suggestion is the authors can elaborate more on the specific architectural or algorithmic advantages of GS-LSS.

4. I also suggest expanding the "Efficiency Trade-off" (Page 14, Section IV.5) to provide a more nuanced discussion of the computational overhead vs. the benefits, especially for different application contexts.

5. Some recent 3D Gaussian Splatting research needs to be cited, e.g.:
StylizedGS: Controllable Stylization for 3D Gaussian Splatting, IEEE TPAMI 2025
Gaussians on Fire: High-Frequency Reconstruction of Flames, ECCV 2026
SSD-GS: Scattering and Shadow Decomposition for Relightable 3D Gaussian Splatting, ICLR 2026

Reviewer: 3

Recommendation: Author Should Prepare A Minor Revision

Comments:
My overall assessment is positive. The paper studies a relevant problem and presents a reasonable solution. Aggregating geometric information across training views gives the optimization additional guidance beyond the loss from an individual image. Combining this guidance with adaptive stochastic updates is well motivated.

I have the following suggestions：

1. Add more implementation details. These include training iterations, network widths, and CPF update frequency. Please also specify the sampling scale, view splits, and random seeds. A configuration table and code availability statement would help replication.

2. Check the reported numbers. Table III gives 16.951 dB for MatrixCity, while Table V gives 16.693 dB. Please explain any differences in settings. The stated gain of 0.468 dB also seems inconsistent with Table III, which gives 0.726 dB.

3. Check the sign in Eq. (13). Based on the definition of δ(g), the right-hand side appears to need a minus sign.

4. Discuss when inaccurate training geometry may weaken CPF guidance. A short example would help clarify this limitation. Also, Table VII tests stability in only one setting. Please keep the conclusion within this scope.
